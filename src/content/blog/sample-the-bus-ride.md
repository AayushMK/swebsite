Nobody hands you a rulebook when you step onto a crowded bus, and yet everyone follows one. You don't make eye contact for too long. You shift your bag without being asked. You pretend not to hear the phone call happening six inches from your ear.

Psychologists call this kind of thing a social norm, but that phrase makes it sound tidier than it is. On the bus it feels more like a dance where nobody admits they are dancing.

## The person who breaks the rule

Every ride has one. Someone talks loudly, or takes two seats, or stands exactly where the door opens. What interests me is not the person, but the rest of us. The sighs. The glances that travel from passenger to passenger, checking: are you seeing this too?

> We rarely punish rule-breakers directly. We just make sure everyone else knows we noticed.

That small, silent alliance between strangers might be the most honest moment of the whole journey.

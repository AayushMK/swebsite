There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over a room when a sad film ends. Nobody wants to be the first to speak. And yet, a week later, we go looking for that feeling again.

It seems irrational. Why would anyone volunteer for heartbreak on a Friday night?

## Sadness with a safety net

One explanation is that a film lets us feel something enormous without any of the consequences. The loss is real for two hours, and then the lights come on and our own lives are still where we left them.

Another is that sad stories make us think about the people we have. You walk out of the cinema and text someone you haven't spoken to in a while.

I think both are true, but I also think there is something simpler underneath: being moved reminds us that we can be.
